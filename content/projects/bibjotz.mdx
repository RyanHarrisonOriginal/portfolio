problem: |
  Free-form notes feel flexible and then fall apart. A thought about a passage loses the passage. A guide gets revised and the old reflections pretend they were written against the new one. Study becomes a pile instead of a record.

  BibJotz is built around guides, drafts, and references so study can change without becoming disposable.
invariantBody: |
  A reflection is not a loose note that happens to mention a verse. It points at a passage, and it points at the version of the guide that was in force when it was written.

  When the guide changes, earlier reflections remain true to the guide they were made with. They are not silently rewritten to match the latest wording.
contracts: |
  - A reflection references a passage, not a pasted copy that can drift.
  - A guide is versioned. A reflection names the version it was written against.
  - Editing a draft does not erase the previous draft.
  - The public reading surface and the private study surface do not share a writable record by accident.
architecture: |
  The web app, the study API, and the Bible API are separate repositories. The drawing should show which one owns identity, which one owns text, and which one is only a client.

  Stack listed on this sheet is taken from the current build: Next.js, PostgreSQL, and Tailwind CSS. Treat anything more specific as not yet written.
failureModes: |
  - A guide edit that rewrites history instead of cutting a new version.
  - A reflection that stores passage text inline, then disagrees with the source.
  - Two drafts of the same study with no way to say which one is current.
  - A reference that resolves for the author and fails for the next reader.
outcome: |

change: |
  The versioning rule is the part to pressure first. If a guide can still change under an existing reflection, the invariant is prose and not a system.
